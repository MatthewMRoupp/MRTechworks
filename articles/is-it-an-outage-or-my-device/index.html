Is It an Outage, Your Internet, or Your Device?

MR Techworks · Updated November 13, 2026

When something suddenly stops working, identifying which part of the connection failed can save hours of unnecessary troubleshooting.

1. Try another trusted website or application

If one service fails but other sites load, the problem may be limited to that service. If nothing loads, check your internet and device connection first.

2. Compare another device

Try the same task on a second phone or computer. If all devices fail similarly, the issue could be your router, internet provider, or the online service. If only one fails, focus on that device.

3. Test Wi-Fi against mobile data

If available, disconnect your phone from Wi-Fi and test on cellular data. A working cellular connection but failing home Wi-Fi suggests a home network or internet-service problem. A problem on both networks suggests, but does not prove, a provider-side issue.

4. Check the official service status

For work or school Microsoft 365, an administrator can sign in at admin.microsoft.com and open Health → Service health. For Spectrum internet, sign in to the Spectrum outage-support page to check the service address. Other providers have similar official support resources.

5. Avoid repairs that cannot fix an outage

Do not repeatedly reset an otherwise correct password, remove your work account, reinstall Outlook, or reset your computer just because a cloud service is unavailable. These steps may create additional work without fixing the incident.

6. Troubleshoot locally if no outage explains it

  1. Record the exact error and when it began.
  2. Restart only the affected application.
  3. Check that other sites and devices work.
  4. Restart the device when appropriate.
  5. For business devices, ask your IT administrator before changing accounts or security settings.

If a problem persists after the provider reports recovery, local troubleshooting may still be needed. MR Techworks can help narrow down the cause.
