# MR Techworks editorial sourcing and media permissions

Last reviewed: November 13, 2026.

## Written coverage
- Facts about product specifications, availability, dates, outages, cybersecurity incidents, laws, prices and claims beyond common general knowledge must be supported by dated, clickable references from official sources or trustworthy reporting. Credit the publisher and named author when applicable.
- Use original MR Techworks prose and practical analysis. Quote verbatim only when the words themselves are informative, use quotation marks or blockquotes, and name the speaker/writer, publication and source URL. Keep quotations brief and proportionate.
- A hyperlink and credit are not a blanket license to copy and republish a full article. Copy longer text only if the copyright holder explicitly grants the relevant reuse rights and honor all limits and conditions of that license.
- For historical stories, distinguish the date an event occurred from the date this site's retrospective was published. Correct false or stale claims with dated revisions.

## Photographs, screenshots and art
- An image on a news publisher's page is not automatically reusable. Credit is not permission. Before copying it, verify an actual license or press-media reuse grant that authorizes this site's commercial/editorial use, or obtain permission. Do not hotlink a publisher's photograph as a workaround.
- Use original photographs, explicitly permitted stock imagery (e.g. Pexels under its license), CC0 assets, and appropriately attributed Creative Commons images. Follow license terms separately for every photo and any adaptations.
- For CC BY, show the creator, clickable original image page, linked CC license and modification statement. For CC BY-SA, additionally honor share-alike terms on the adapted image. If the license prohibits commercial use, excludes derivatives, or is ambiguous, skip the image unless appropriate permission is obtained.
- Pexels permits commercial website use with credit optional, but the creator's credit is appreciated; don't imply an identifiable person or depicted brand endorses MR Techworks.
- Prefer actual hardware photographs for product explainers where rights are clear. Generic gamer or email photos must be described as illustrative, never actual gameplay, a particular outage, a specific device error or proof of an incident.
- Keep a record of image path, article URL, creator, source, license, alt text and modifications. Show visible credit at the image or in an accessible linked credits section, and add descriptive alt text.
- Avoid publishing broken image paths: upload and verify images before changing article hero images, library thumbnails, social-preview metadata or structured data.
- Retain necessary copyright notices and attribution in metadata when feasible. Optimize large files for the web without removing rights information that must be preserved.

## Workflow
1. Verify and cite the story's underlying facts.
2. Identify suitable image candidates from official press sites or third-party collections, then verify each asset's rights. If unclear, use the site's approved generic art instead.
3. Assemble a licensed source/credit manifest. Download only images that may lawfully be used; do not claim files have been downloaded until verified.
4. Prefer 16:9 optimized WebP and useful alt text. Preserve attribution and share-alike conditions on derivatives.
5. Publish images and page references in the same reviewed change where possible. Verify live 200 responses and credits.
6. Review scheduled news updates for stale information and any changes in image rights.
