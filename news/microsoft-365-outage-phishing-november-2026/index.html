Security advisory

Fake Microsoft 365 outage emails target users after service disruption

Published November 18, 2026 · Campaign observed November 13 · Microsoft research published November 16

Microsoft Threat Intelligence reports that attackers sent phishing messages disguised as Microsoft 365 service-health notices after the November 12 service disruption. The messages claimed that email had been held and would be deleted within 24 hours unless recipients clicked a recovery link.

What Microsoft confirmed

Microsoft observed the campaign on November 13 and published its analysis November 16. The links redirected to non-Microsoft websites. Microsoft assessed the activity as likely credential phishing, but said its evidence did not confirm stolen credentials, malware delivery or subsequent account compromise. The campaign does not establish that Microsoft 365 is currently experiencing another outage.

What to do if you receive one

  1. Do not use an unsolicited email's restore-messages button.
  2. Open the Microsoft 365 admin center directly to verify service health, or ask your organization's administrator.
  3. Report the suspicious email using your organization's established phishing-reporting process.
  4. If you entered your password, immediately notify IT. Administrators should reset affected credentials, revoke sessions, review MFA registrations and inspect sign-ins and inbox rules.

Microsoft says Defender for Office 365 blocks or quarantines messages associated with this campaign, but organizations should still investigate suspicious clicks and messages.

Sources and uncertainty

Primary source: Microsoft Security Research, November 16, 2026. Independent reporting: Computerworld, November 17, 2026. This report describes a documented phishing campaign, not a verified new outage or a confirmed local Corning/Elmira compromise.
