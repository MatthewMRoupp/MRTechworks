SECURITY ADVISORY

ScreenConnect Attack Campaign: What MSPs and Customers Should Know

MR Techworks · Drafted November 16, 2026 · Microsoft research published November 13

Microsoft details SCKit attacks abusing compromised ScreenConnect environments. Learn practical checks for IT teams and remote-support customers.y was reported beginning November 11, 2026; the initial intrusion route remains under investigation.

What investigators have confirmed

Microsoft's November 13 research describes a campaign involving cloud-hosted ScreenConnect instances used by managed service providers. Malicious commands could install an additional remote-access client on customer endpoints. SCKit also attempts to persist and target technician sessions, potentially widening access to other managed devices.

What is still uncertain

ConnectWise is investigating whether an RMM+ extension authentication weakness contributed to initial access. That is not a confirmed entry route for every incident. The existence of this campaign does not mean all ScreenConnect installations are compromised.

Practical steps for IT teams

Review ConnectWise's advisory and extension inventory; disable affected RMM+ extension variants as directed; verify technician ScreenConnect clients are version 26.6.5 or newer; inspect remote-command and Toolbox history; and check endpoints for unauthorized clients, persistence and suspicious sessions. If compromise is suspected, investigate through a separate trusted management channel rather than reconnecting through the potentially affected instance.

Why ordinary customers should care

Remote-support tools are powerful by design. Customers should verify unexpected support sessions with their provider and report unfamiliar remote-control prompts. A legitimate support product can be abused without the software itself being inherently malicious.
