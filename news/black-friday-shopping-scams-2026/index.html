Black Friday 2026: Spot Fake Deals, Stores and Reviews

MR Techworks · November 24, 2026

Black Friday falls on November 27, with Cyber Monday on November 30. Security researchers are already seeing seasonal scams. A few minutes of checking can save you from a bad purchase or stolen account.

What researchers found

On November 23, Kaspersky reported more than 19 million blocked phishing attempts involving shopping, banking and payment services during January through October. These are detections within its own network, not a count of individual victims. Its release and detailed report give inconsistent year-over-year figures, so we are not quoting a growth rate.

Bitdefender's November 19 report separately documented imitation stores, fake promotions and delivery-related lures observed during the early shopping season. Malwarebytes' November 17 research also describes convincing fake storefronts and reviews. These reports cover different samples and cannot be added together. No specific Corning or Elmira incident is established by them.

Five checks before you buy

  1. Open the store yourself. Use a known address or official app rather than a link in a text, ad or unexpected email. Check the actual seller on marketplaces.
  2. Compare the exact model. Match model number, capacity, condition and warranty. Check historical prices where available and compare the total including shipping. A crossed-out $200 price with a $100 sale is not necessarily a $100 saving if the item normally sells for $110.
  3. Read reviews critically. Look for independent testing, specific product details and recurring complaints. A verified purchase badge alone is not proof of authenticity.
  4. Protect checkout. Prefer a credit card when practical, review dispute protections and avoid sellers demanding gift cards, cryptocurrency or irreversible transfers. Use unique passwords and multifactor authentication.
  5. Verify delivery notices. Open your order history or carrier app directly instead of paying a fee through an unexpected message.

If you already entered information

Contact your card issuer using the number on your card. Change exposed passwords from a trusted device, sign out other sessions and enable MFA. If you installed unknown software or allowed remote access, disconnect the affected device from the network and seek assistance. Report suspected fraud at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

Before replacing a slow computer or purchasing a new mesh Wi-Fi system, check whether an upgrade would solve the problem. Contact MR Techworks for help evaluating equipment in the Corning and Elmira area. No affiliate links are used in this guide.
