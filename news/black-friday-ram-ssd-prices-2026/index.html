Black Friday RAM vs. SSD: Buy Now or Wait for 2027?

MR Techworks · Published November 25, 2026 · Market outlook checked November 25

Computer memory and solid-state storage can both make a system more useful, but their price outlooks are not the same. As Black Friday approaches on November 27, industry research points to continued pressure on RAM prices and a possible downward turn for NAND flash in 2027. That does not promise cheaper retail SSDs next week, or even next quarter.

First, what is the difference?

RAM holds the data your computer actively uses while programs run. If you frequently run out of RAM, the system may slow down or constantly swap data to storage. An SSD stores Windows, applications, games and documents even when the computer is off. Moving from a mechanical hard drive to an SSD can noticeably improve startup and loading times, but adding an SSD does not solve a RAM shortage.

An industry note attributed to Bank of America, reported by Tom's Hardware on November 18, suggested consumer NAND contract prices could fall 5–10% in Q1 2027. That is a separate estimate, not a guaranteed discount on the SSD you buy online. On November 25, TrendForce reiterated a potential NAND downward cycle while expecting DRAM supply tightness to persist. Read the dated sources below.

Should you buy this Black Friday?

Buy to solve an actual problem, not because the banner says “50% off.” A computer booting from an older hard drive can benefit from a compatible SSD now. If your drive is failing or nearly full, waiting months for a possible contract-price correction may not be sensible. If your 1 TB NVMe drive is healthy and has plenty of free space, a larger drive can usually wait unless the current price is genuinely good.

For RAM, 16 GB is a useful target for many everyday computers, and 32 GB may help with heavier multitasking, creative applications and certain games. Moving to 64 GB or more should be driven by measured workloads, not a vague promise of future-proofing. Check memory usage in Task Manager first; some laptop RAM is soldered and cannot be upgraded.

A five-step checklist before buying

  1. Identify the exact computer and part. Record its model and existing RAM/SSD specifications. DDR4, DDR5, SO-DIMM, desktop DIMM, SATA and NVMe are not interchangeable.
  2. Check compatibility. Confirm supported memory generation, capacity, number of slots, M.2 length, interface and any firmware or warranty restrictions.
  3. Compare the same SKU. Check capacities, performance, endurance rating, warranty, included heatsink and whether the seller is reputable. A 2 TB “sale” price can be misleading if it was the normal price last month.
  4. Protect your files first. Back up important data and locate any BitLocker recovery key before replacing or cloning a system drive. If the existing drive is failing, prioritize safe data recovery over repeated write-intensive operations.
  5. Include the total cost. Shipping, tax, any tools or adapters, and installation labor matter. Compare repairing or upgrading with replacing the whole computer.

What should you wait on?

Optional capacity upgrades are generally the easiest to defer. By contrast, a system that routinely exhausts RAM, uses a slow mechanical boot drive or shows signs of storage failure may justify a purchase now. The right answer depends on current performance and the total cost, not an uncertain 2027 forecast.
