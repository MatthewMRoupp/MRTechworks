Black Friday Home Tech Upgrades Worth Considering in 2026

MR Techworks · Published November 27, 2026 · Editorial research checked November 27, 2026

The best home-technology deal is the one that solves a real problem. A newer router, faster SSD or smart device can be useful, but not if it replaces perfectly good equipment or requires unexpected installation work. Here is how to decide what deserves your budget.

1. Fix weak Wi-Fi before paying for faster internet

If a wired computer gets the speed you expect but an upstairs laptop struggles, your problem may be wireless coverage rather than your internet plan. Start by testing Ethernet and Wi-Fi separately. Before buying a mesh system, check whether its nodes can use Ethernet backhaul, whether it has enough ports and whether your walls and layout make a wireless connection practical. A three-pack is not automatically better than two well-placed access points.

Read our home Wi-Fi basics and mesh versus extenders versus Ethernet guide before buying.

2. Upgrade a slow computer intelligently

An SSD can be a meaningful replacement for a mechanical boot drive. More RAM helps only when your actual workload needs it. Check the machine’s service manual for supported memory type, drive interface and maximum capacity; some thin laptops cannot be upgraded. Compare a repair with the cost of a reliable replacement. Back up files and locate BitLocker recovery information before touching the boot drive.

Memory and SSD pricing forecasts are not identical. See our November RAM and SSD outlook, then compare the specific part number rather than a broad category.

3. Buy a real backup, not just more storage

An external drive is useful for a second copy of important files, but a drive that remains connected all the time can also be affected by malware, theft or power events. Consider more than one backup copy and keep at least one off-device or off-site. Check capacity, connector type, backup software and whether you can actually restore a test file. Read our 3-2-1 backup guide.

4. Smart thermostats and connected devices

ENERGY STAR states that certified smart thermostats are independently assessed using field data for energy savings. That does not mean every thermostat works with every heating system. Verify HVAC wiring, required common wire, heat-pump compatibility and whether your Wi-Fi reaches the thermostat. For cameras, doorbells and smart plugs, check ongoing cloud subscription fees, privacy settings and whether basic functionality continues without an internet connection.

5. Printers, docks and other practical upgrades

For a printer, compare ink or toner cost per page, duplex printing, scanning, front trays and photo handling—not just the low hardware price. For laptop docks, confirm the laptop's actual USB-C, USB4 or Thunderbolt display capabilities and the number of independent external displays supported. A cable with the right connector shape does not guarantee the feature you need.

A five-minute purchasing checklist

  1. Write down the specific problem and your budget.
  2. Record the current model number and compatibility requirements.
  3. Compare an exact model across established sellers and check prior prices.
  4. Add taxes, subscriptions, cables, mounting gear and labor to the total.
  5. Keep a copy of the receipt, return terms and warranty documentation.
