Windows 10 in 2026: Extended Security Updates and Your Options

By MR Techworks · Published November 13, 2026

Original event: October 14, 2025 — regular Windows 10 support ended

Windows 10 computers did not stop working when regular support ended on October 14, 2025. The change is about ongoing security support. Microsoft's consumer Extended Security Updates program offers eligible owners additional time to plan a safer transition.

What end of support means

A Windows 10 PC can still open applications and store files, but standard security updates, new features and ordinary Microsoft support have ended for affected consumer editions. That makes continuing to use an unprotected installation a growing risk.

End of support does not automatically justify discarding a functional computer. The right question is whether it can be upgraded, kept temporarily protected through ESU, or replaced at a sensible cost.

What the consumer ESU program provides

Microsoft's consumer ESU program supplies eligible Windows 10 version 22H2 systems with critical and important security updates. It does not include ordinary feature improvements or comprehensive technical support. Microsoft's current consumer program page lists coverage through October 12, 2027.

The enrollment options include syncing PC settings at no additional cost, Microsoft Rewards points, or a one-time paid enrollment in supported circumstances. Verify the options presented on your computer; rules and local availability may change.

How to check enrollment

On a personal Windows 10 device, open Settings, Update & Security, and Windows Update. Install applicable updates and look for the ESU enrollment link. Microsoft also documents a licensing preparation update, KB5126256, needed for eligible enrollment; installing it alone does not activate ESU.

Business-managed, domain-joined and certain MDM-managed devices do not qualify for the consumer workflow. Consult the organization's IT administrator for the appropriate business licensing.

Plan the transition

Check whether your hardware qualifies for Windows 11 and whether the programs you rely on are compatible. Back up files, save any BitLocker recovery keys and compare repair costs with replacement value before changing an operating system.

ESU is a temporary security bridge, not a permanent substitute for a supported device. Revisit Microsoft's current guidance before enrolling or making a purchase.
